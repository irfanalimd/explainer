Raft: How servers that can crash keep one shared log
level core · 3 stories · 5 knobs (Servers, Client commands, Message delay (ms), Lost messages, Message timeline)

HOOK      Five servers keep copies of the same list, and any of them can crash. How do they never
          end up disagreeing?
GOAL      Keep one list of commands identical on every server, even when servers crash or the
          network splits.
OBJECTIVES
  o-elect: Explain how one leader is chosen: a random timer, then votes from a majority, at most one
           leader per term.
  o-commit: Predict when an entry is committed: once the leader hears that a majority, not every
            server, stores it.
  o-survive: Explain why a committed entry survives the leader's crash: any two majorities share a
             server, and voters refuse a less complete log.
  o-split: Predict what a leader cut off with too few servers can commit, and what happens to its
           entries when the network joins up.
  o-own: Explain why a new leader writes an empty entry of its own term before older entries can
         count.
PICTURE   a group keeping one shared notebook
  copy    each person's copy of the notebook  =  a server's log  (log)
  line    one line in the notebook  =  an entry: one command and its term  (entry)
  taker   the elected note-taker  =  the leader  (leader)
  round   which vote this is  =  the term, an election round number  (term)
  most    “most of us have copied it”  =  a majority, such as 3 of 5  (majority)
  counts  a line that counts  =  a committed entry  (commit)
  here    the note-taker's “still here”  =  a heartbeat  (heartbeat)
  breaks  People in one room hear each other at once. Servers learn only from messages, which can
          arrive late or never, so a cut-off leader can go on believing it leads.
BELIEFS
  m-all: “An entry is safe only once every server has stored it.” A majority is enough: any later
         majority shares a server with it, so the entry can never be outvoted.
  m-written: “An entry is safe as soon as the leader writes it in its own log.” Alone, the leader
             can crash and take it along. It counts only once a majority stores it.
  m-lost: “When the leader crashes, its entries are lost and the client must send them again.” A
          committed entry sits on a majority, and only a server holding it can win the next vote.
  m-boss: “Whoever is leader can make an entry count, and the one that led first wins.” A leader
          needs a majority to commit. Cut off from it, it commits nothing, and a newer term's leader
          decides.
WORDS
  leader: (the note-taker) The one server that decides what goes into the list. The others copy what
          it sends.
  log: (a copy of the notebook) A server's list of commands, in order. Every server keeps its own
       copy.
  majority: (more than half) More than half of all the servers, such as 3 of 5 or 4 of 7.
  term: (an election round) An election round number. Each new election raises it, and a newer round
        beats an older one.
  entry: (one line) One line of the list: one command, such as x←3, with the round it was written
         in.
  client: (the program sending commands) The program outside the servers that sends commands, such
          as x←3, to be saved.
  commit: (make it count) Made final once most servers store it. A leader counts copies only of its
          own round's entries.
  heartbeat: (a “still here” message) A short message the leader sends often, so no one starts an
             election.
  partition: (a split in the network) A break in the network that leaves groups of servers unable to
             hear each other.

   RECAP
     1. The servers elected one leader, by majority vote.
     2. The leader wrote each command into its log and sent copies.
     3. An entry counted once the leader heard that most servers had it: it was committed.
     4. In the end every server held the same entries, in the same order.
   TAKEAWAY  A leader commits an entry of its term once most servers store it. Any two majorities
